# M1: A world to explore

The work items of [M1](../ROADMAP.md#m1-a-world-to-explore), in order, and the
decisions they share. The roadmap keeps the goal and exit criterion. This
spec is deleted when M1 ends; [Afterwards](#afterwards) lists what moves where.

M1 builds the mechanisms, not complete content. The biome table, the generator
and the set of logical asset IDs keep changing after M1.

## Shared decisions

**Biomes are content.** The engine never names a biome. Generation classifies
tiles with a biome table: each entry has a stable name, the conditions on the
fields that select it, its map colour, its obstacles and, later, its logical
asset IDs. The game binary supplies the table, as it supplies the input
bindings. It moves to content files when the `content` crate exists.

**The world is generated once, completely, at genesis.** The world is finite,
so the generator builds all of it before the first tick, not chunk by chunk on
demand. Later iterations can then add passes over the whole world, such as
rivers, erosion or rain shadow. Parallel generation must give the same result
as sequential generation.

After genesis, the terrain is data. No code recomputes it from the seed or
samples the noise again to find out what a tile holds. A future save therefore
stores the terrain, and stays valid when the generator changes. The world keeps
the seed and the generation parameters only to record where it came from. A
tile stores the index of its biome in the table; a save will store the stable
name, so that changing the table never breaks a save. Presentation may still
derive purely visual variety, such as which grass sprite a tile shows, from its
position.

**Presentation shares the terrain without copying it.** The world keeps each
chunk behind a shared, immutable pointer, and the snapshot carries the set of
pointers. When farming or building later change a chunk, only that chunk is
replaced (copy on write). A snapshot's cost therefore does not grow with the
terrain, and presentation never sees a half-changed chunk.

**Fields are quantised.** Elevation, temperature and moisture are `u16`s
spanning the generator's range. They keep tiles and future saves small, and
the terrain hashable and exactly comparable, which floats are not.

**Obstacles are a tile layer, with footprints.** In M1, an obstacle (a rock, a
tree, a fallen log) is part of the terrain, not an entity. Its footprint, the
tiles it blocks, is content: offsets from its anchor tile, one tile by default.
The anchor tile holds the obstacle, and each other tile of the footprint holds
the offset to it. A footprint may cross chunk boundaries and the seam, never the
north or south edge. It is unrelated to the sprite: a tree covers about 3×3
tiles but blocks only its trunk. The simulation never reads a sprite's size, so
swapping placeholders for the pack blocks no other tile. Whether obstacles
become entities, as buildings will, is decided in M2 or M3; footprints and
placement carry over either way.

**Placeholders cover all content; the pack covers part of it.** Every logical
asset ID has a placeholder, so content is never limited to what the pack can
show. The pack covers a subset of the IDs, and that subset changes when its
author or we add art. An ID without art, such as the ground of a desert biome,
shows its placeholder. Placeholders follow the pack's format: blob blocks in
its 12×4 layout, seasonal variants, animation frames and sprite proportions.
Swapping in the pack therefore needs no code change. Placeholders do not follow
the pack's content. They must be
[readable enough to play with](../../docs/VISION.md#decisions-so-far), across
the whole set, including what the pack does not show.

**Generation stays easy to change.** It will see many iterations, so:

- The rest of the game sees only the terrain. The passes and their intermediate
  data stay private to the generator.
- Each pass reads earlier fields and writes new ones. Adding, replacing or
  reordering a pass changes only the generator.
- Its parameters are a struct with no defaults, supplied by the game binary.
- Its tests check properties only: no golden maps, no expected biome
  proportions, no values at a fixed seed. Code that reads terrain is tested on
  small hand-built terrain and never calls the generator.

## Work items

Each item is one pull request, or two if it grows. Items 4 and 5 can swap.

### 1. Survey the asset pack

**Done.** The answers are in [the findings note](../findings/asset-pack.md).

### 2. World shape

**Done.** Wrapping is `Grid` in `core`. Chunks of 32×32 tiles, footprints and
shared terrain are in the `terrain` crate, filled for now with a placeholder
pattern in `sim`. A world's size is configured in chunks, so it is always a
whole number of them. Where the width is even, two columns half a world apart
are equally far both ways; the offset then goes west whichever tile comes
first. This item also took on two pieces of later ones: interpolation across
the seam, from item 3, and keeping the wanderer off obstacles, from item 4.

### 3. Seeing the world

- **Batched coloured tiles**, each in the colour of its biome. The per-frame
  cost depends on the visible area, not on the world size.
- **The camera wraps.** It no longer clamps `x`, and keeps its centre canonical
  so that precision holds over a long pan. Where the view spans the
  seam, both sides are drawn, with their entities. A view wider than the world
  shows it repeated. Interpolated positions are already canonical, and a step
  across the seam is already drawn as a one-tile move.
- **Tile inspector** in the debug overlay, for the tile under the cursor:
  canonical coordinates, chunk, fields, biome and obstacle.

**Tests:** a screen point converts to the canonical tile on either side of the seam.

**Done when** you can pan across the seam both ways without a jump, the wanderer
crosses it smoothly, and the inspector shows canonical coordinates on both sides.

### 4. Generation v0

- **Seamless noise**, written in-house. It samples a cylinder (`x` becomes an
  angle) so that no seam shows.
- **Fields:** elevation; temperature from latitude, elevation and noise;
  moisture from noise.
- **Biomes** from the biome table, whose conditions can use all three fields:
  water can be a biome below a sea level. The inspector shows the biome's name.
  The default table is not limited to the biomes that
  [the pack can show](../findings/asset-pack.md#what-the-art-can-show).
- **Obstacles** for each biome, with a density from the table, placed only where
  their whole footprint is free and on an allowed biome. The default content
  has a multi-tile obstacle, such as a fallen log, so that no code assumes one
  tile. Water is impassable.
- **The wanderer** starts on a passable tile; it already never steps onto an
  obstacle. Creating a world with no passable tile returns an error.
- **Debug views** in the overlay colour the map by biome, elevation,
  temperature, moisture or obstacles.
- **Regeneration** from the overlay, with a new seed or new parameters: a
  development action that starts a new world, not a command inside a run.

**Tests**, properties over many seeds and small worlds:

- the same seed and parameters give the same terrain;
- every tile has a biome from the table (every field value is valid, since
  fields are quantised);
- no two footprints overlap, and every footprint tile allows its obstacle;
- across the seam, the fields change no more than between other neighbouring
  columns;
- parallel generation, if any, gives the same terrain as sequential generation;
- on average, the rows near the poles are colder than those near the equator:
  the only climate property, since the roadmap asks for latitude.

**Open:** the default world size. Measure generation time before choosing.

### 5. Asset pipeline

- **Logical asset IDs** and their naming scheme. The biome table names the IDs
  of its ground and obstacles.
- **Placeholder spec.** A committed file gives each logical ID a recipe, with
  one colour, or one colour per season. `cook` draws each seasonal variant as
  its own region. The rest of the recipe depends on the kind of asset:
  - A terrain has a pattern from a small fixed set, such as solid, stipple,
    hatching or waves. From it, `cook` draws the 47 blob tiles in the pack's
    12×4 layout. An animated terrain also has a frame count, and `cook` shifts
    the pattern from one frame to the next.
  - A sprite has a shape from a small fixed set, such as a canopy over a
    trunk, a boulder or a long capsule, drawn with an outline and a ground
    shadow. It also has a size and an anchor. Where the pack has an
    equivalent, the size follows its proportions (about 3×3 tiles for a large
    tree, 8×2 for a fallen log), so that occlusion and culling behave as they
    will with the pack.
- **`cook`** builds the runtime container: atlases, and the mapping from logical
  IDs to regions. A terrain maps to a blob block, a sprite to a rectangle and an
  anchor. Each region has a frame list and seasonal variants.
- **Layered terrain.** Terrains have an order. For each layer of a tile, the
  renderer picks the blob tile from the layer's 8-neighbour mask, across the
  seam too. Animated tiles show their first frame in M1.
- **Obstacle sprites**, often larger than a tile, are drawn once, at their
  anchor, in order of `y` with the entities, then of `x` and a stable key.
  Culling keeps a sprite anchored outside the view whose image reaches into it,
  using the largest overhang, which `cook` computes.
- **Validation at load.** Loading fails, naming them, if content names
  logical IDs that the container lacks.

**Tests:** over all 256 neighbour masks, a corner counts only when both of its
edges are present, giving 47 distinct tiles; a mask at the seam uses the wrapped
neighbours; a sprite anchored outside the view but reaching into it is drawn; packed regions lie inside the atlas and never
overlap; a missing logical ID fails validation.

**Done when** the game runs with the placeholders alone and, at every zoom
level and without the inspector, you can tell apart:

- each biome of the default table from the others, also in a greyscale
  screenshot;
- passable tiles from impassable ones;
- the tiles that an obstacle blocks from the other tiles that its sprite covers;
- the entities from the terrain.

**Open:** when placeholders are cooked. A fresh clone must run with `just game`
alone, and stale cooked output must be impossible. Recommended: `cook` is a
library and a binary; the game cooks the placeholders in memory at startup, and
the pack is cooked explicitly with the tool.

### 6. Calendar and seasons

- **Calendar in `core`**: the day, time of day, season and day of the season,
  from the tick and the configured lengths of a day and a season.
- **The overlay** shows the date.
- **Seasonal variants.** The renderer chooses each tile's and sprite's variant
  from the season of the snapshot's tick. An asset without variants never changes. In M1
  the season affects only presentation. The placeholders of the default
  content have seasonal variants, so that a fresh clone also shows the seasons
  change.

**Tests:** values worked out by hand at the boundaries: the last tick of a day,
the first tick of a season, the first tick of a year.

**Open:** how to watch the seasons change, since a season takes hours at 1×.
Recommended: a command-line option for the day length, for very short days in
development runs.

### 7. The real pack, locally

- **Configuration** locates the pack and our own art: our modifications of the
  pack and our additions in its style. Both stay outside the repository.
- **The provenance manifest** is committed: source, download date, checksum and
  licence. The pack has no version number, so the checksum covers every file.
  `cook` refuses a pack whose checksum differs.
- **The region mapping** is committed (it holds no image data). It covers the
  pack and our own art, and `cook` builds the container from it.
- **Fallback.** `cook` resolves each logical ID from our own art first, then
  from the pack, then from the placeholders. It lists the IDs that fall back
  to a placeholder; a fallback is not an error. A container cooked from the
  pack therefore holds every ID that has a placeholder.
- **Credit** to the author in the README, as the licence requires.
- CI never needs the pack, and git ignores the cooked output.

**Tests**, on small hand-built inputs, without the pack: an ID that our own
art and the pack both lack resolves to its placeholder; our own art takes
precedence over the pack.

**Done when** the game runs locally with the pack, with transitions and
seasonal variants, and shows the placeholder of each logical ID that has no
art. It still runs from a fresh clone with the placeholders alone.
